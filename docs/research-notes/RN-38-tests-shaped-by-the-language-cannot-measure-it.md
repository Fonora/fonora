# Tests shaped by the language cannot measure it

## Research Question

[RN-37](/research/notes/interrogatives-need-dimensions-not-values) found that the interrogative family had almost no test coverage, and that a malformed construction could be fluent, logged as a gap, and still pass CI. That was reported as a local discovery about one construction family. This note asks whether it was local at all, or whether the measuring instruments are built in a way that guarantees blind spots.

The question matters because the project's confidence rests on two suites: a 1001-phrase golden corpus reporting 66 percent clean coverage, and 42 structural probes gating regression. If either is shaped by the language it measures, then a green suite is evidence of consistency rather than evidence of expressive range.

## Hypothesis

If the golden corpus was generated by a model instructed to avoid constructions the language cannot express, coverage measured against it is circular: the pass rate is high partly because the failures were never written down. If the structural probes exercise a different engine than the one that ships, probe results carry no information about production behavior.

Both conditions predict the same signature: a defect that is severe in meaning, fluent on the surface, honestly recorded in the gap baseline, and invisible to CI. The strong form of the hypothesis is that at least one such defect is present right now, and that it can be found by auditing the corpus for what it does not contain rather than by reading what it produces.

## Approach

The corpus was audited by counting constructions instead of inspecting outputs, on the reasoning that a suite's blind spots live in its absences. Every count in this note comes from `data/fonoran-translation-tests.json` directly rather than from a prior report.

The generator prompt was then read to see whether the absences were incidental or instructed. The probe runner was traced to determine which engine it calls. Finally the most severe predicted defect was fixed end to end, as a test of whether the blindness explanation actually held.

## Evaluation

Construction frequency across the 1001 phrases:

| Construction | Phrases | Status in the language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `and` coordination | 90 | works, 89 render correctly |
| `or` disjunction | 10 | inverted meaning, now fixed |
| progressive aspect | 39 | collapses to `move` |
| `who` / `which` relative | 38 | Open |
| `more` / `less` quantifier | 16 | works as a quantifier |
| `if` conditional | 1 | `von` particle exists |
| `than` comparative | 0 | no construction |
| `because` | 0 | no construction |
| `why` | 0 | works, and did before RN-37 |

The pattern is not random. The four constructions with the least coverage are among those the language handles worst, and two of them have literally zero coverage. `tools/fonoran-stranger-corpus-generate.js` explains why: its instruction block contains "NEVER use how questions" and, until RN-37 edited it, "NEVER use why or how questions (those concepts are intentionally absent from the target language)". The corpus was built to avoid the language's weak points, and it succeeded.

The probe runner was then traced. `tools/fonoran-translation-probes.js` imports `translateEnglish` from `tools/fonoran-translator.js`, where that name is an alias for `translateEnglishLegacy`, carrying an explicit `@deprecated Use translate() from fonoran-translate.js` marker. All 42 probes therefore run the rule-based engine, not the LLM compiler that serves `/api/fonoran/translate`.

The two engines diverge substantially on the same input. Given *are you tired or sick?* the LLM path groups both alternatives into one slot, `object: ["tired","sick"]`. The rule-based path scatters them into `event: [tired]` and `object: [sick]`. Given *do you want food or water?* and *do you want food and water?* the rule-based engine returns `be sak tel ye` for both, so it cannot distinguish conjunction from disjunction even in principle.

The predicted defect was found in the disjunction set. All ten `or` phrases rendered alternatives as bare juxtaposition, which is the identical surface form used for `and`:

| English | Rendered | Reads as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| That person is a friend or an enemy. | `ba guba gamba` | person friend enemy |
| Are you tired or sick? | `be nes femtam du` | you feel tired sick |
| Is the pain here or there? | `tes nam tak` | pain here there |
| Is the baby a girl or boy? | `fa dehu` | both alternatives gone |

## Findings

**Juxtaposition licenses conjunction and cannot license disjunction.** This is the language finding, and it explains the lopsided numbers. Bare juxtaposition is a correct strategy for *and*: a reader recovering `mi nes mak saklo` for "I am cold and hungry" reconstructs the conjunction without help, which is why 89 of 90 corpus cases are fine and the single failure also carried an unrelated lexical gap. The same strategy fails for *or* in every case, because the reader has no way to recover exclusivity. The asymmetry is a property of what juxtaposition can mean, not a shortfall in coverage.

**The failure inverted meaning rather than losing it.** This distinguishes it from every gap in the 132-entry baseline. `ba guba gamba` does not under-specify whether the person is a friend or an enemy; it asserts both, which is the opposite of a choice. Two phrases were worse still: *is the baby a girl or boy?* and *do you mean this or that?* lost both alternatives entirely and returned fluent strings anyway.

**Honest gap tracking and dishonest output coexisted.** `or` was correctly listed in the gap baseline the whole time, so the project's records were accurate. What the records could not convey is that the rendered surface was clean, well-formed, and confidently wrong. Genuine lexical gaps surface visibly as bracketed forms like `[tetsas]`; this one surfaced as a normal sentence. Anyone reading output saw success, and only the baseline held the correction. Gap counts measure what failed to resolve, not whether what resolved is right.

**The information needed for the fix was already present.** The cached LLM frames carried both alternatives grouped in a single slot and the connective recorded in `unresolved`. Nothing had been lost during parsing. The defect was entirely in rendering, which is why the repair required no new model calls and no re-translation: the frames had been right, and had been right for as long as the cache existed.

**The repair was already in the lexicon.** Coordination and disjunction are quantification over a coordinated set, and the quantity domain already holds the operators: `lu` (one, "a single one"), `mel` (all, "every one of them"), and `ket` (some, "part of them"). Closing the group with `lu` yields `guba gamba lu`, "friend enemy, a single one", which a reader who knows only `lu` can decode. Per Design Rule 0 this stays lexical, costing no particle, no new root, and none of the six remaining prefix-safe root forms. RN-37 asked whether the quantity domain's lack of a dimension root was a defect; this note is a partial answer, in that the domain's values turn out to do grammatical work the inventory never advertised.

**Two test layers are blind in complementary ways.** The corpus omits the constructions the language cannot express, by instruction. The probes exercise an engine that does not ship. Neither layer could have caught this defect: the corpus contains only ten relevant phrases and accepted their output as golden, and the probe engine cannot represent the distinction being tested. The 66 percent clean figure is a consistency measure, not a coverage measure, and true coverage of unrestricted English is lower by an unknown margin.

**A negative result worth recording: the fix cannot be ported to the rule-based engine.** A parallel implementation was written for the legacy path and then deleted after testing showed it could never fire. That engine never groups coordinated items into one slot, not even for bare "food or water", so there is no group for a marker to close. The blocker there is constituent grouping, not coordination, and code that looks live while being unreachable is worse than absent code.

## What Changed

Disjunction is expressible. `applyDisjunction` in `tools/fonoran-llm-grammar-brief.js` closes a coordinated group with `lu` when the frame reports a lost connective and a slot actually holds the alternatives. It runs alongside the other deterministic frame repairs at render time, so the model performs semantic parsing while the grammar rule stays deterministic, consistent with RN-36 and RN-33.

Eight of the ten corpus phrases now mark disjunction and the goldens were refreshed accordingly. Measured coverage moved from 663 clean phrases to 670, or 66 percent to 67, with passing tokens up from 4822 to 4830. Seven of the eight became fully clean; the eighth, *we can go together or stay here*, still reports `can`, which is the modality gap and the next item of work. The other two are unchanged on purpose, and both fail for reasons unrelated to coordination: *is the baby a girl or boy?* reports `girl` and `boy` as gaps because neither has a root, and *do you mean this or that?* keeps `or` as a gap because demonstratives are discarded as function words. The rule deliberately declines to fire when the alternatives were lost upstream, since marking a lone item would manufacture a false reading, which is the error this whole note is about.

The construction is documented in `docs/fonoran-grammar.md` under Rule 4, including the positional rule that distinguishes it from the existing *alone* idiom. `lu` before a concept quantifies that concept, as in `lu de`; `lu` after a group of two or more closes that group. Marking only ever attaches after what it closes, so the two readings never compete.

Four disjunction probes were added, all marked broken, each carrying the reason. They are not broken because the rule fails in production; they are broken because the probe engine cannot group alternatives. Recording that distinction in the failing test is the point, so the next reader finds the engine mismatch attached to the evidence rather than only in this note.

`docs/fonoran-gap-assessment.md` was written as a standing layer-by-layer assessment against descriptive-linguistics criteria and named comparators, including the finding that Fonoran's structural profile is a pidgin and that the features it lacks are close to the set creolization supplies. It is the reference document for the roadmap that follows from this note.

One process finding also belongs here, because it recurred. A debugging request against the running server rewrote a cached frame and broke a golden, the same trap RN-37 hit. Any live translate call can silently overwrite cache state that goldens depend on. The cache was restored and the suite re-verified, but the underlying hazard is unfixed: the write path treats a debugging call and an intentional warm identically.

## Open Questions

- Should the probes run both engines and label which is which, or should they simply point at the production compiler? The second is cleaner but discards whatever regression value the rule-based path still has as a fallback.
- Is the rule-based engine worth maintaining at all? It is deprecated, it diverges materially from production, it cannot group constituents, and it is the only consumer keeping 42 probes pointed away from shipped behavior.
- Should the corpus gain an adversarial level that deliberately includes `than`, `because`, `or`, and modals? It would lower the headline pass rate, which is the argument for doing it.
- Does the conjunction and disjunction asymmetry extend to the other connectives? `but`, `nor`, and `so` are all in the same discard set, and `but` in particular carries contrast that juxtaposition cannot express either.
- Modality is the next and largest hole at roughly 131 phrase hits. The `sak` precedent suggests ability, necessity, and permission can be ordinary roots chained in the Action slot, and corpus counts suggest three senses rather than four English words. Does the same "the operator is already in the lexicon" result hold there, given that `ha` (rule) is glossed "how things must be done"?
- Should a live translate call be forbidden from writing cache entries unless explicitly warming? The failure is silent, it has now happened twice, and it converts a debugging step into a golden regression.
